How it works

Three steps from a practice session to a race plan.

  1. 1

    Measure in practice

    Drive a few clean laps on race fuel. Note your average lap time, the fuel you used per lap, and how many percent the tyres lost over those laps.

  2. 2

    Enter your race

    Fill in the race length, tank size, pit stop times and your drivers. Fields that do not apply to your race hide themselves.

  3. 3

    Race to the plan

    The stint sheet tells you when to box, how much fuel to add, how many tyres to change and who gets in. Copy it and share it with your team.

The maths

No hidden tricks. This is everything the planner does with your numbers.

Stint length

A stint is as long as the fuel allows: tank size divided by fuel per lap, minus your safety margin, rounded down.

90 L ÷ 3.6 L = 25 laps
25 − 1 lap margin = 24 laps

Pit stop time

Pit lane loss plus the time you stand still. Standing still is refuelling and the tyre change, together or one after the other, plus a driver swap. Changing 2 tyres takes 6 seconds, all 4 takes 12.

86.4 L ÷ 2.5 L/s = 34.6 s refuelling
28 s lane + 34.6 s + 8 s swap = 71 s

Tyres

Tyres lose the same percentage every lap. A stop changes 2 or 4 tyres. A pair gets changed when it would drop below your lowest condition during the next stint.

6% in 3 laps = 2% per lap
24 laps = 48% worn, 52% left

A limited number of tyres

With enough tyres you never go below your lowest condition. With too few, the changes are spread over the race so the longest run on one pair is as short as possible.

The plan tells you how low the tyres go, and in which stint.

The finish

The race ends on the first lap that finishes after the clock reaches zero. The last stop only adds the fuel you need to get there, so it is shorter.

6 h race, last lap ends at 6:01:12

What is left out

Traffic, safety cars, weather, damage and slower laps on worn tyres are not in the model. Keep a safety margin and adjust during the race.

The plan is a starting point, not a promise.

Questions

Where is my data stored?

In this browser, on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere, and there is no account. If you clear your browser's site data, your setup is gone.

Why does the plan keep fuel in the tank?

That is your safety margin. Fuel use changes with traffic, slipstream and driving style. One lap of margin means a slightly thirsty stint does not leave you stranded on the last lap.

Why does a stop sometimes change only 2 tyres?

This happens when you set a tyre limit that is too low to change all four every time. The planner then changes the most worn pair, so the tyres you have last the whole race as evenly as possible.

Why is the last stint shorter than the others?

The race ends on time, not on a number of laps. The last stint only runs until the first lap that finishes after the clock reaches zero, and it only takes the fuel for those laps.

My numbers in the race are different. What now?

Change the inputs and the plan updates straight away. A mode that re-plans from the current lap during the race is planned for a later version.

Is this an official tool?

No. It is a fan-made planner and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the makers of Le Mans Ultimate.
